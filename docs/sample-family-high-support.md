## Getting started

Teresa Ruiz, mother. Writing about Daniel Ruiz, who goes by Danny.
Address: 2115 Alder Court, Vienna, VA.

## About Danny

**Who he is.** Danny is 24, built like his grandfather, and the most reliable
person in this house. He has autism and an intellectual disability, and those
words tell you almost nothing useful about him. He knows every bus route in
Fairfax County and whether they are running late. He has worked the Saturday
shift at Harris Grocery for three years and has never once been late. If he
tells you he will do a thing, the thing gets done, exactly, forever.

**The history that shaped him.** Danny was born in Arlington in 2002. He was
diagnosed at two and a half. He didn't speak until he was six, and the first
thing he said was the number of the bus that goes to his grandmother's house.
Middle school was the hard chapter: a placement that didn't fit, two years we
are still sorry about. Everything turned when he got to the county transition
program and met Mr. Okafor, who saw that Danny doesn't learn from being told,
he learns from being shown, once, slowly. His grandmother died in 2020 and he
still points out her bus stop every time we pass it.

**First five minutes with a stranger.** Tell him your name and give him a
minute. Don't fill the silence; he is deciding about you, and he is allowed.
If he shows you the bus schedule on his phone, you are in. Never touch his
backpack. It is not a rule we made, it is a rule he made, and it is absolute.

**What strangers get wrong.** People talk to him louder and slower than they
need to, or they talk to whoever is next to him instead. He understands
nearly everything said at normal speed. He simply may not answer, and his not
answering means he is thinking, not that he didn't hear.

**What he cannot abide.** Being hurried. The word "maybe." Anyone touching
his backpack. Plans that change without a warning and a reason.

## A typical day

**Mornings.** Danny wakes at 6:40 without an alarm. Bathroom, then he checks
the bus feed on his phone before anything else; do not ask him anything until
he has. Breakfast is two eggs scrambled hard and toast cut corner to corner.
Meds go in the green cup by his plate. He leaves for the program at 8:05, the
8:12 bus, and he likes to be at the stop four minutes early.

**Evenings.** Home at 4:30. One hour alone in his room with the door shut;
this hour is not optional and not a sign anything is wrong. Dinner at 6.
Shower at 8:30, and the towel goes on the left hook or the shower has not
happened. Lights out at 10 with the fan on, even in winter.

**Sleep.** Solid, once he is down. If he is up past 11 something is wrong;
check tomorrow's schedule with him, there is usually a change in it he heard
about and didn't mention.

**The fixed points.** Program weekdays 9 to 2. Harris Grocery Saturdays 9 to
1. Mass with Carmen Sunday 10:30. Bus museum the first Saturday of the month,
which is the appointment of his life.

**Sensory.** Fire alarms and air dryers are genuinely painful for him.
Fluorescent hum bothers him before anyone else can hear it. His ear
defenders are in the front pocket of the backpack; offering them is always
welcome, putting them on him is not.

**A good day** ends with him narrating the bus delays to us at dinner. The
narration is the tell. A quiet Danny at dinner is a Danny with a problem he
has not found words for yet.

**A hard day** starts with an unannounced change. He goes quiet first, then
rigid, then loud. The order never varies, and quiet is the moment to act.

## Communication

**How he communicates.** Short sentences, four or five words. He answers
questions reliably about concrete things and struggles with "why" questions.
For anything hard he uses the picture cards in his backpack, a laminated
ring of about thirty. He made them himself with Ms. Okafor and adds to them;
the newest card is how you find out what he has been worrying about.

**Yes and no.** Yes is "yeah" with a nod. No is silence and looking away.
Pressing after the silence gets you a louder no and costs you an hour of
trust. Silence IS the answer.

**Pain.** He will not tell you something hurts. He guards the spot and stops
eating. A skipped meal is a fever, a bad tooth, or worse, every single time
it has ever happened. Check teeth first; he has a high pain threshold and a
bad molar history.

**What helps.** "First, then" sentences: first the store, then the bus. Real
times. Writing a change on the whiteboard where he can see it coming.

**What to avoid.** "Maybe." "Later." "We'll see." "Calm down." Sarcasm reads
as lying to him. Never promise a bus ride you cannot deliver.

## Health and medical

**Conditions.** Autism, intellectual disability (moderate), anxiety.
Constipation is chronic and serious for him; it changes behavior a week
before anyone would guess the cause.

**Allergies.** Peanuts, serious: hives and vomiting within the hour, no
airway involvement so far. Benadryl from the kitchen cabinet, and call
Carmen. Everything he eats at the program is packed from home.

**Medications.** Sertraline 50 mg, morning, for anxiety; never stop it
abruptly. Miralax nightly cap in juice; he knows it is in there and it is
fine. Melatonin 3 mg at 9:30 if he asks for "the sleep one."

**Providers.** Dr. Priya Chandra, family medicine, (555) 014-6210, who he
trusts and who books him the first slot of the day. Dr. Weiss, dentist,
(555) 014-6244; sedation cleanings, twice a year, book months ahead.

**Preferred hospital.** Fairfax Northern. His records and his behavior plan
are on file, and they have a quiet intake room if you ask for it.

**How appointments work.** Someone he knows goes in with him, always. He
answers the doctor's questions honestly but minimally, and he will say
nothing hurts while guarding his jaw. The escort's job is to say what the
week has actually looked like.

**Insurance.** Virginia Medicaid, and secondary coverage through Miguel's
employer plan. Plan names only here; where the cards live is in the money
section.

**What worked.** Sertraline, genuinely; the difference was two months and
unmistakable. Visual schedules. Sedation dentistry.

**What didn't.** Risperidone: weight gain, flat affect, no benefit, and he
was the one who told us, with a picture card he made that said "foggy."

## Behavioral support

**Triggers.** Unannounced changes. Fire alarms. Being touched from behind.
Crowded waiting rooms. The phrase "calm down."

**Early warnings.** He goes still and stops answering. Then he squares his
shoulders and plants his feet. You have about two minutes from the stillness,
and the stillness is easy to miss if you don't know him.

**What helps.** One person talks; everyone else steps back and stops looking
at him. Name the plan in first-then words. Offer the ear defenders. Give him
the walkway, never a corner. It takes ten minutes and it works.

**What makes it worse.** Touch. Crowding. Two people talking at once. Taking
the backpack "somewhere safe."

**Crisis plan.** True crises are rare, roughly twice a year. Call Carmen,
then Miguel. The county crisis line number is on the fridge and in Carmen's
phone. He has never hurt anyone; he wants distance, not a fight.

**For police and first responders.** Danny is a large man who may not answer
questions, may rock, and may walk away from you. That is autism, not
defiance or intoxication. He cannot answer "what's your name" under stress
but will show you the ID card in his left jacket pocket if you ask for it
slowly. One officer, no lights, no touching. He is registered with the
Fairfax County voluntary registry.

## The emergency plan

**If he is missing:** he is not hiding, he is riding. Check the 2A bus
first, then the bus museum. He follows routes when overwhelmed. Call Carmen
while you check; do not wait an hour to take it seriously.

**If he eats peanuts:** Benadryl 50 mg from the kitchen cabinet, watch his
breathing, call Carmen. Call 911 if his lips swell or his breathing changes.

**Call 911 when:** breathing trouble, a fall he doesn't get up from, or any
time he says or shows the "hurt" card and you cannot find the cause.
Otherwise call Carmen, (555) 014-2280, any hour.

**Work.** Harris Grocery, Saturdays 9 to 1, carts and stocking. His badge

## Home and daily living

**Now.** With us, in the house he has lived in since he was four. His room
is his territory and cleaning it is his job, done to his own exacting and
slightly alarming standard.

**Support level.** He manages hygiene with a posted checklist, cooks three
specific meals with supervision on the stove, and cannot manage money beyond
a twenty. He should never be alone overnight; not because of danger, but
because a disrupted morning with no one to steady it unravels his whole day.

**Waiver.** On the DD waiver waitlist since 2019, priority category two.
Carmen manages the annual paperwork; the county letter comes in March and
must not be missed.

**Hopes for his future home.** A supported apartment near Carmen, with his
own kitchen and a bus line he approves of. Two roommates maximum. He has
told us this himself, in his own words, several times, and he means it.

**Hard limits.** Never a large facility. Never out of Fairfax County away
from the routes he knows and the people who know him. Never a placement
that treats his hour alone after program as isolation to be corrected; it
is regulation and he built it himself.

## Legal and decisions

**Guardianship.** We deliberately did not pursue full guardianship. Danny

as supporters, and Teresa holds medical and financial powers of attorney.
He decides his days, his money to twenty dollars, and his own room. Medical
and money above that are decided with him, not for him.

**Who decides what, in practice.** Daily life is his. Medical: explained to
him with the picture cards, decided with Teresa in the room. Money: Carmen.
Anything about where he lives: everyone, slowly, with him first.

**Advocates.** Support coordinator: Ms. Green, Fairfax CSB, (555) 014-7788.

**What we fought for.** The 2019 waiver category appeal: won with letters
from Dr. Chandra and Ms. Okafor. The phrase that moved the wall was
"institutional level of care." Get every denial in writing. Never accept a
no by phone.

## Guidance for the trustee

**What the money is for.** A life, not a ledger. It is for the supported
apartment when his name comes up. It is for the aide who will ride the new
bus route with him until it is his. It is for sedation dentistry without
anyone weighing it against the electric bill, and for the bus museum
membership every single year, forever.

**Easy yeses.** Anything under three hundred dollars that touches buses,
the museum, his kitchen, or Harris Grocery. Respite for whoever is caring
for him; that money protects him, not just them.

**Spend or preserve.** Spend. The house will fund the far future. The trust
exists so his twenties and thirties are not a waiting room.

**Extra scrutiny.** Any request that comes through Ray. Any program with a
beautiful brochure that cannot explain its staff turnover.

**When his wishes and his safety disagree.** Danny's wishes are usually
about sameness, and sameness is usually safe, so this comes up less than
you would think. Where it matters: he would ride buses alone at night, and
the answer is no, explained, with an alternative, every time. Let him take
risks that bruise. Not the ones that break.

**Consult first.** Carmen, always. Ms. Okafor about his days. Dr. Chandra
about anything medical. And Danny. He understands more than the meeting
thinks he does, and he can tell when he was not asked.

## For whoever steps in

**The first week.** Do not change anything you do not have to. Call Ms.
Okafor so the program knows there is a new person; call Ted at Harris so
Saturday holds. Meals from the list on the fridge. Learn the bus feed app
before you need it. Everything financial can wait two weeks; Carmen has it.

**In hindsight.** We waited three years too long to start the waiver
paperwork, and we spent Danny's teens preparing for the person we feared he
would be instead of the person he was becoming. Start everything earlier
than feels necessary, and bet on him.

**Never change.** The hour alone after program. Saturday at Harris. The
first-Saturday museum trip. The backpack rules. These look like quirks and
they are load-bearing.

**Consult before anything irreversible.** Carmen and Miguel together, and
Danny himself, with the cards, with time.
